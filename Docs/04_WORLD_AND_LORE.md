# World, Locations, Tone, Story Hypotheses

## Locked setting
A small town in the late 1970s or 1980s, surrounded by forests. Real supernatural events. Nostalgia, melancholy, mystery, psychological dread, strange/absurd local life; no requirement for constant horror.

## Working geography (not final)
- Roadside **motel** — first playable slice; hallway, rooms, reception, guest ledger.
- **Diner** — familiar town routine; locals, reports, changed names.
- **Local radio station** — archive, announcer, signal source, town conspiracy leads.
- **Radio tower on the hill** — looming background landmark, late-game possibility.
- Optional: newspaper office, telephone booth, old woods, community noticeboard.

## Working case
A local radio presenter vanished during a broadcast, possibly saying "Please don't turn off your receivers." Journalist arrives to investigate. Some residents show awareness of unusual signal behaviour and may help conceal it. Still a **hypothesis**, not locked lore.

## Competing explanation candidates
1. Signal maintains a stable alternative town after an old catastrophe.
2. Broadcast edits collective memory and official records; erased witnesses persist off-signal.
3. Town oscillates between contradictory versions of events; signal suppresses one.
4. Radio signal selectively stabilizes identity, not just physical town.

Do not present all as simultaneously true. Prototype should avoid revealing origin.

## Protagonist open questions
Journalist is preferred, but motivations, home town connection, whether another identity exists, or supernatural identity change are all undecided. Avoid choosing a psychiatric diagnosis by default.

## Atmosphere/art
Painterly 2D backgrounds, side-on street scenes, parallax layers, cinematic framing, expressive character silhouettes, warm diner contrasted with cold fog, vintage 1980s typography and hardware. Reality-shift variations should be recognizable as **the same physical place** rather than entirely separate sets.

## Audio bible
- Stable signal: local ads, jingles, weather forecast, friendly presenter, radio hiss.
- OFF/drift: ordinary ambience becomes uncanny, missing beats, distant voices, distorted tape, isolated footsteps.
- Entity encounter: recognizable motif that recurs when its true witness role is revealed.
- Motel: fluorescent hum, rain, vending machine, intermittent static.
- Save/record: audible mechanical switch / short tape tone; recording is an identifiable fiction event.

## Narrative promise
Not every figure is hostile. Safety may protect an unethical status quo. Don't force "all supernatural events are hallucinations" resolution; user specified real supernatural events.

## Signal geography (WORKING, do not reveal lore early)
The broadcast is hypothesized to influence **the entire town**, while carried receivers may locally stabilize a person's perception and immediate space. A stationary powered set may create a wider/stronger pocket of normality. In areas outside consistent coverage, radio use could be less reliable. Do **not** choose transmitter science, exact radius, or an explanation for cognitive effects before a gameplay test.

## Horror without death
The tension is being **forgotten, misled, stranded in a changed corridor, or unable to trust an unrecorded clue**, not bodily injury. Anomalous witnesses may appear aggressive without wanting to hurt anyone. A stable broadcast might perpetuate the very conspiracy the journalist is examining.

## Naming / location
**89.4** is the preferred working game title. It can be the local frequency shown on a dial and repeated in jingles; its deeper narrative meaning is open. No final town name selected. `DEAD AIR` may remain a Git/repository codename during transition.
